# Streamed pairs are recognised from the head, and other pairs stay buffered up to a threshold

Marasi reads every body whole before the modifier chains run, which breaks gRPC, server-sent events and any other traffic that only works while it flows. We decided that a direction of a request/response pair is carried as **stream messages** only when its own head names a **message framing**: `application/grpc*` (`grpc`), `application/grpc-web*` (`grpc-web`), `application/connect+*` (`connect`), and for responses also `text/event-stream` (`sse`) and `application/x-ndjson` or `application/jsonl` (`ndjson`). Such a pair is a **streamed pair**. Its stored body is empty and its stream messages, each with its own UUID, hold the content; hooks, Checkpoint, injection and events work on stream messages the way they work on WebSocket messages, and holding one stops reading only that direction. The path and the body never make a pair streamed, and unary Connect (`application/proto`, `application/json`) stays an ordinary pair.

Every other pair keeps the buffered model that the earlier phases built, with one way out. Its body is forwarded as it arrives, can no longer be held or edited, and is stored up to the threshold with a too-large ending once the head declares a length over the threshold, the buffered body passes it, or the body stalls with no new bytes for 2 seconds after some arrived. The threshold defaults to 64 MiB, the WebSocket message limit, in a config key of its own. A Checkpoint hold or Lua body edit that this skips is named in a proxy log rather than left to hang.

**Considered options:** stream every body and make every body hook per-chunk; stream every body unless Checkpoint or a hook wants it. The first throws away whole-body inspection and editing for ordinary traffic. The second cannot be decided at the head, because whether a hook wants the body is known only after the hooks run, so the wire would depend on the hooks and differ between the conformance runs.

**Consequences:** a streamed direction that declares a length commits to it before its messages are read, so an edit, drop or injection that would change the total length is refused, not re-framed. An ordinary pair that passes the threshold is the only ordinary pair a tester cannot hold or edit, and its stored body is truncated.
